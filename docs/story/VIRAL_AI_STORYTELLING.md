# What makes viral AI-animated story content work (2025–2026)

Scope: dialogue-driven animated story channels (MSA / My Story Animated style, Dhar Mann-style morality dramas, now widely reproduced with AI animation), Resilia-style first-person dramas (8 transcripts studied in `STORY_FORMULA.md`), and viral AI 3D story shorts (micro-drama, "toon stories" loop shorts).
Method: the 8 Resilia transcripts in this folder plus 2025–2026 creator and practitioner write-ups (sources at the end). We borrow **mechanics only**: no hype, no fake claims, no melodrama beyond what a money topic supports.

## 1. Hook patterns (first 1–3 seconds decide)
- **Conflict already in progress.** Open on the wound, not the setup: "My brother's getting married in June to my ex-wife." / "What did you say?! You hid this from me…"
- **Anti-common-sense line.** A sharp line that is socially or emotionally unexpected stops the scroll.
- **Open loop + promise** at 3–5 s: "He has no idea what that night cost him." The viewer is told a payoff exists.
- The picture changes within the first 2 s; the first spoken line is ≤12 words.
- For CFA: the hook is a **money moment with a clock** (a send stuck, a code request, a declined purchase, a missing balance) spoken **by a character**, not the narrator.

## 2. Line length
- Character lines are mostly 4–15 words; reactions are 1–4 words ("Wait. It's gone?"). Explanations come in 2–3 short causal sentences, then another character interrupts.
- Narration (where used) is a single short framing sentence: time, place, consequence.
- Nobody delivers a paragraph. Monologues over about 35–40 words kill retention in dialogue formats.

## 3. How often they cut
- Shorts body: a new shot or reframe every **3–5 s** (a tighter crop of the same shot counts). The opening often has a burst (several setups in the first 4 s), then holds a shot during dense action or emotional dialogue.
- Cut on pauses in the voice so picture and sentence move together; in dialogue, cut on speaker change (shot / reverse shot) and hold for a reaction.
- Long-form (5–10 min animated stories): about 4–8 s average shot length, with re-engagement peaks around 25%, 50% and 75% of runtime.

## 4. Dialogue vs narration balance
- Dialogue-driven animated dramas run about **70–85% character dialogue**. The narrator (if any) frames time and place, bridges scenes and lands the final reflection.
- Resilia uses first-person narration, but every turning point is carried by a quoted line from another character. The turn is always *heard*, never summarised.
- **CFA rule:** characters carry all conflict and all explanation in first person; the narrator (Bill) is a sparse third-person, past-tense frame and never a participant.

## 5. Cliffhangers and reveals
- **Failed obvious fixes** before the solution (two attempts that don't work) make the answer feel earned.
- **Reframe reveal:** a mentor names the hidden mechanism ("it isn't you, it's the network"). The reveal flips the meaning of the hook.
- **Mid-point cliffhanger** around 50%: a new obstacle or deadline ("It says pending. The shop closes in ten minutes.").
- The ending pays off the cold open with a callback line. A series can close on a light hook into the next episode.

## 6. Emotional beats
Wound → stakes with a clock → (backstory in one-line fragments) → failed tries → mentor reframe → small concrete fix → visible progress counted ("first test: arrived. Second: arrived.") → relief → callback. Real, everyday stakes ($50, rent, a cousin's shop) beat melodrama.

## 7. Music swell
- Low bed under dialogue (about 18–22 dB below the voice), almost no music in the hook. **The swell lands on the reveal or turn**, then dips back under the solution dialogue. A soft resolve closes on the callback.
- In AI micro-dramas, a sting or hit on the hook line and a riser into the cliffhanger are common.

## 8. Retention tricks
- Open loops renewed every 20–40 s ("…but that wasn't the problem").
- Name and repeat one concrete object (the green notebook, the $50).
- Countdowns and clocks; counted progress.
- Captions always on (many viewers watch muted). Use **different caption styles for the narrator and the characters** so viewers know who is speaking.
- Distinct, consistent characters with recognisable speech patterns; viewers return for people, not for topics.
- No intros, logos or greetings; the lesson arrives as the solution, never as a lecture.

## How this is folded into the tool (PR "story")
- **Planner (SYS_PLAN):** hook type, the mid-point cliffhanger, two failed fixes, the reveal line, emotional beat map and callback.
- **Critic (SYS_REVISE + read-aloud):** checks the hook (≤12 words, conflict, spoken by a character), line length, narrator purity, distinct voices, cliffhanger present, swell-worthy turn, and story logic / accuracy against the primer.
- **Director (SYS_DIRECTOR):** cuts every 3–5 s (shot/reverse-shot on speaker change), narrator lines over wides, establishing shots or B-roll, character lines as on-screen speaker shots, the swell marked on the reveal, and narrator captions styled differently from character captions.
